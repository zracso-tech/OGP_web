The sales process has a structure. From the moment a lead is born to the moment they sign, five phases go by that everyone recognises. But here's the problem: they don't all need the same thing. Some need structured, automated process. Others need people at their absolute best. And confusing the two costs you sales.

## Phase 1: Capture

A lead comes in. Someone has to check whether it already exists in the CRM, find the right email across three different systems, cross-reference LinkedIn data with your database, classify the company by sector and size.

**This is where AI comes in.** All of that, automated in thirty seconds: data enrichment, deduplication, scoring. While the machine does it, your salesperson does something else.

Gain: 5 hours a week per salesperson.

## Phase 2: Routing and prioritisation

Who do you assign this lead to? Some salespeople close better with startups. Others with large enterprises. Some talk better to CTOs, others to CFOs. Deciding this by hand is chaos.

**This is where AI comes in.** Smart assignment based on history: the machine knows who closes more with each type of client and distributes leads automatically.

Gain: 3 hours a week. Your best people stop wasting time arguing over whose lead it is.

## Phase 3: The first conversation

The salesperson talks to the client, uncovers their problems, takes notes. Afterwards they have to log it, summarise the call, update the CRM with the key points.

**This is where AI comes in.** Automatic transcription while you talk, a summary of the key points, CRM tagging. All happening in the background.

Gain: 2 hours a week per salesperson. Nobody loses half an hour after every call writing up what just happened.

## Phase 4: The proposal (this is where it stops)

The client is waiting for your proposal. They want to see that you understood their specific case, that you thought about them, that you understood their pain and how you can help. And they expect you to tell them so.

AI prepares the context: compiles pricing, analyses data, builds the structure. But the narrative — the "why this, for you, now" — you write that yourself. That's what sets a salesperson apart from a bot. You can let it help you design how to present the case; you bring the strategy and the knowledge.

Gain: the machine saves you two hours compiling data. You spend those two hours thinking about what will actually convince them.

## Phase 5: The close (this is where it stops too)

The client says yes or no. They negotiate the price, ask for changes. You have to read the silences, tell whether the objection is real or just an excuse, provide reassurance.

AI prepares the ground: an executive summary of the whole prior conversation, weak points, the reasons they said yes at earlier stages. The salesperson walks in prepared.

Gain: the salesperson has all the information pre-chewed. But the final decision — the close, the relationship that holds it together — is human.

## The real maths

Automating the right phases adds up to thirty hours a week of admin work that simply disappears. That's real space back on your calendar to sell. It's not that the machine closes better deals. It's that you finally have room to breathe.

Five hours hunting for data, three organising assignments, two summarising calls, ten more on automated follow-ups: thirty hours that weren't selling anything.

Win them back and focus on what actually moves your conversion numbers. Not because the product got better — because your salespeople had time to sell.

## The question that matters

Where is your team right now? In the phases the machine can handle? Or handing the machine the one thing only a human can close?

That's the difference between innovation and wasted time.
